The drill

You're on the floor. A patient is changing.

Post-op deterioration is rarely a single number — it's a pattern that builds. Each case hands you the bedside, then drills the four moves that catch a sick patient early:

1 · Recognise 2 · Respond (BTF) 3 · Act 4 · Communicate (ISBAR)

Every choice gets a constructive, encouraging rationale — you build judgement either way — and each debrief shows how the complication connects to the others, the web you'll start to see at the bedside.

Designed against automation bias

A decision tool that just hands you the answer makes you stop thinking. This one is built to keep you accountable: you commit your own call first while the adjunct stays silent, you must tick the data you\'ve personally checked, and the adjunct then shows a "can be wrong" accuracy figure, flags when your read and its read disagree, and asks a question rather than declaring a verdict. It supports the decision — it never replaces you.

      Likelihood lab

      A diagnosis starts as a pre-test probability (your gut feeling from the handover). Each finding carries a likelihood ratio — how much more often it shows up with the disease than without it. Stack the findings and watch the post-test probability move. Toggle them and see it for yourself.

      Suspected diagnosis: Pulmonary embolism

      15%

      Reading LRs: LR > 10 strongly rules a diagnosis in; LR < 0.1 strongly rules it out; LR ≈ 1 is a useless test. And remember — probability isn't priority: a dangerous diagnosis gets worked up even at a modest probability.

      Likelihood ratios here are rounded, illustrative teaching values to build intuition — not validated decision-rule figures. Use real tools (e.g. Wells score, PERC, age-adjusted D-dimer) in practice.
      Socratic drill · probing first principles (after Bohr)

      Drill outward through the orbits: the further out, the deeper the reasoning — Orbit 2 recognise, Orbit 3 trajectory, Orbit 4 synthesis. State the principle in your head before you reveal — the answer usually ends with a harder question.
