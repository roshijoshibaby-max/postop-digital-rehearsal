Designed to promote independent clinical reasoning▾

This educational adjunct promotes active clinical reasoning by requiring learners to formulate an initial clinical impression and verify personally assessed findings before receiving structured feedback. It then presents an alternative interpretation, highlights areas of discordance and uses reflective questioning to encourage further analysis. The adjunct supports—but does not replace—independent clinical judgement or established escalation processes.

      Structured Evidence-Weighted Differential Reasoning

      Evidence-weighting lab

      Your level of concern starts from the handover, then each finding shifts it up or down. Some findings strongly increase concern, some reduce it, and some don't really distinguish anything. Toggle the findings and watch the current level of support move — this shows direction and strength, not a calculated probability.

      Reading the evidence: a finding can strongly increase concern, mildly increase it, not distinguish much, or make a diagnosis less likely. And remember — support isn't priority: a cannot-miss diagnosis is still worked up and escalated even when support is modest.

      This lab organises findings by direction and strength of support to build reasoning intuition. It is an illustrative teaching aid — it does not compute a validated probability. Use real tools (e.g. Wells score, PERC, age-adjusted D-dimer) and senior judgement in practice.
      Socratic drill · probing first principles (after Bohr)

      Drill outward through the orbits: the further out, the deeper the reasoning — Orbit 2 recognise, Orbit 3 trajectory, Orbit 4 synthesis. State the principle in your head before you reveal — the answer usually ends with a harder question.
